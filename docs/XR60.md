# The XR-60 Phantasos

The sixth research craft, and the first that owes nothing to an aeroplane. The XR-50 Morpheus is the last machine
built from engines: fans, ramjets, a rocket, a wing that sweeps. The XR-60 is built from a field. Its drive is a ring
of plasma, its lift comes out of its skin, its fuel is metallic hydrogen, and it is flown from a pod on the ground
by a pilot who sees through its sensor crystal. It is meant to look like something that was not made here: a knife
of black glass with forward-curved blades and a ring of light at its tail. It is the XR-50 several times over in
everything that makes a research craft quick: three times the thrust for its weight, three times the pull, three
times the roll, Mach 12 where the XR-50 stops at 7, a hover with no moving parts, and a surge that triples all of it
for forty seconds. This note is the design and the record of the decisions behind it. Where it and the code
disagree, the code wins: fix the note in the same change.

## What it is, in a page

- **A blade, not a body.** A knife-edged double-chined lifting body 16.6 m long, faceted like a cut stone, with a
  needle nose and a sensor crystal where a canopy would be. Forward-swept scimitar blade wings grow out of its aft
  flanks and curve forward and up to tips that fold down 70 degrees for the hover (span 12.4 m unfolded). Twin
  all-moving fins canted 35 degrees out, a ventral keel that is the scramjet's duct. No canopy, no inlet lips, no
  nozzles: only the ring.
- **A ring drive.** The tail is an annular magnetoplasmadynamic thruster 2.2 m across: six petals around a ring that
  spins at 1,400 rpm, accelerating its plasma to 90 km/s. From a standstill it gives its full 2,100 kN; it needs no
  air (in the atmosphere it takes some through the annulus, air-augmented), so it gives the same thrust at Mach 0 on
  the ground and in the near-vacuum at 80 km. Its petals vector the stream 35 degrees in pitch and yaw.
- **An MHD-bypass scramjet.** From Mach 2.5 the ventral keel's duct lights: a magnetohydrodynamic generator ahead of
  the combustor takes energy out of the inflow (slowing it, so it burns), and the accelerator behind puts it back into
  the exhaust. Its combustors stand 160 kPa of dynamic pressure, and it carries the craft to Mach 12.
- **Metallic hydrogen.** 2,600 kg of metastable metallic hydrogen is both the ring's reaction mass and the energy
  store: its reversion gives 216 MJ/kg, twenty times a hydrocarbon. A compact aneutronic core (proton-boron) powers
  the fields. The fuel lasts about 25 minutes at full ring thrust and 9 hours at cruise.
- **Surge.** The core charges a bank; dumped into the ring, it triples the drive (6,300 kN) for forty seconds. The
  bank recharges in four minutes. Lit and shut off at will (a double press of A, or X), like the XR-50's rocket.
- **Lift out of the skin.** Four flush ventral field emitters, two ahead of the centre of gravity and two behind,
  hold it up with no moving parts: the lever brings them in. Plasma actuators along the whole wing (circulation
  control, scaled up) raise its lift to seven times a plain wing's. The structure takes 2,000 g either way.
- **A plasma sheath.** The skin ionises its own boundary layer. The sheath halves the drag's friction and stands the
  heat off: the skin, a self-healing refractory metamaterial, is limited at 2,500 °C and warned at 2,350.
- **Field torque.** The ring's vectoring and the tip emitters give it its moments where there is no air to push on;
  the surfaces (the folding tips, the elevons, the fins) where there is. It commands rates: 500 g up and away, 2,400
  deg/s of roll, 600 deg/s of yaw.
- **An operations pod for a cockpit.** Its cockpit view is the pod it is flown from: a sealed hexagonal capsule,
  a wrap of five monitors curving round the pilot (ahead, either side, the chin, the telemetry band), a side stick
  and a throttle, violet light.

## Decisions

### One index, one flight model hook

The XR-60 is the roster's stable index 17 (`kPhantasos`), `special` 4, engine code 8. Every behaviour of its own
lives in `src/aircraft_xr60.cpp`; `Plane::substep` calls `xr60Aero` for its aerodynamics and `xr60Thrust` for its
drive and moments. `thrustAt`, `fuelFlowAt` and `liftThrustMax` ask the same drive model, so the autopilot, the fuel
planner and the learned envelope fly the same machine the pilot does.

### The ring: thrust that needs nothing

The ring's thrust is its rated 2,100 kN at any speed and in any air, scaled only by the throttle (a plasma drive has
no spool: it answers in a tenth of a second) and by the surge. It burns its reaction mass at a rate that gives an
effective Isp of 9,200 s (fiction, as the XR-50's rocket's 5,400 s is): about 23 kg/s at full thrust, so a full
tank is 25 minutes of it, and cruise at a sliver of throttle lasts hours. Its vectoring is 35 degrees any way,
measured about the ring's centre 7 m behind the centre of gravity, so its pitch and yaw authority is enormous
whatever the airspeed, and the fly-by-wire leans on it everywhere the surfaces run out.

### The scramjet and its bypass

Like the XR-50's, the scram thrust is a coefficient on the dynamic pressure the keel captures (1.1 m²) with the
combustors held to a limit, here 160 kPa; the MHD generator is why the limit is higher (it takes a third of the
inflow's energy out ahead of the combustor, so the combustor sees a slower flow, and the accelerator returns it). It
lights from Mach 2.5 and its coefficient falls past Mach 9, to the balance of thrust and drag at Mach 12. The
corridor is the same shape as the XR-50's, a few kilometres higher and twice as fast.

### The heat and the sheath

The leading edges follow the XR-50's law: the recovery temperature against the radiative equilibrium of a Sutton-
Graves heating, over a thermal lag. The sheath (full with the core at power, gone with it off) halves the heating
and the friction drag, and the metamaterial skin's limit is 2,500 °C rather than 1,650. Mach 12 at 30 km is held;
Mach 12 at 22 km still burns it, so the corridor is real and the tiles say which side of it the pilot is on.

### Lift from the field

The four ventral emitters give 0.6 of the ring's rated thrust straight down between them, the lever bringing them in
and the ring's thrust falling by what they take; they are symmetric about the centre of gravity, so the hover is
balanced in pitch by construction, and the fore-aft pair trade thrust for pitch and the left-right pair for roll in
the hover. The circulation field runs from 0.5 at idle to 1 at full power and raises the lift curve and the elevons'
roll power to 1 + 6 times a plain wing's; it closes nowhere (there are no slots to melt: the field is the actuator).
Beneath it the body's compression lift, Newton's, carries on past Mach 4.

### Limits, and the gate past them

The pitch command is the rate of a 500 g pull, 8 to 24 rad/s slow; the roll rate 42 rad/s, the yaw 10.5. The rate
loops are three times the XR-50's stiffness (it has the moments for it). The one load limit is the airframe's: 95%
of the 2,000 g structure either way. The angle of attack is held at 35 degrees until the stick is pulled through its
stop, then post-stall to 90, held there by the ring. The limiter closes the angle's rate in as it nears a limit, as
the XR-50's does, to what the ring can stop there.

### Surge

The bank holds 40 s of surge; lit (a double press of A, or X) it triples the ring's thrust until the bank is spent
or it is shut off; it recharges at a sixth of the rate it spends, only while off. Surge inhibits nothing: the
emitters, the scramjet and the vectoring all stay. The SURGE tile shows the bank.

### The shape

The body is its own signed distance field (`src/shaders/xr60_sdf.glsl`), all of it behind `HAS_PHANTASOS`, so no
other aircraft's program carries it. Its section is a faceted diamond rather than an ellipse: a sharp dorsal ridge,
two chines (the upper one the lifting body's, the lower one the compression surface's edge), a flat keel. The blade
wings are panels whose leading edge sweeps forward 22 degrees from a root at the aft flank; the outer 2.2 m of each
is a narrow claw (a rigid part) raked further forward and curling 0.4 m up to its tip, that drops to 70 degrees for
the hover and the landing and stands as a winglet otherwise. The fins are all-moving, raked 46 degrees and canted 35
degrees out, on the aft shoulders. The keel under the belly is a knife-edged blade of diamond section. The ring is
six petals (rigid parts, vectoring) around a spinning inner ring (a rigid part) at the tail, the body's end a cone
inside it. Seams along the chines, the blades' edges and the keel glow cyan with the drive's power and violet with
the surge; the emitters glow under it in the hover; the crystal in the nose is a faceted gem lit from inside.
Moving parts (IDs 58 to 62): the tip blade, the elevon, the fin, a petal, the ring. Materials 176 to 185: the
obsidian skin with its violet sheen, the knife edges, the crystal, the ring's metal, the seams, the petals' hot
faces, the emitters, the keel duct's inside, the ring's plasma face, the tip lamps.

### The operations pod

Its cockpit is the pod it is flown from (`xr60_sdf.glsl mapXr60Station`), drawn round the eye where a cockpit would
be: a hexagonal capsule 2.4 m across, dark panels with violet strips, a console that wraps round the pilot. Five
monitors (`research_cockpit_layout.glsl xr60Pane`, each facing the eye): the main one ahead and wide, one either side
curved in at 52 degrees, the chin monitor in the console looking down for the hover, the telemetry band above with
the bank, the sheath and the core. A side stick to the right, the throttle to the left, pedals under the console.
Its materials are 186 to 199. The monitors are windows, as the XR-50's are (`cabin_windows.glsl`), with the remote
pilot's symbology on their glass (`plane_screens.glsl stationHud`, the XR-50's symbology in the XR-60's colours); with
the cameras on they show feed rig 4's pictures.

## Flying it

- **F / V** run the emitters in and out: the hover with no moving parts.
- A double press of **A**, or **X**, lights the surge and shuts it off; the bank recharges while it is off.
- The tips fold themselves for the hover and the landing.
- To reach Mach 12, climb as you accelerate: 20 km by Mach 7, 30 km by Mach 11.
- Pull the stick through its stop for post-stall flight; the ring holds it there.
- It needs no air: on the ring alone it leaves the atmosphere.

The research terminal's four cards train each of these: the field hover and conversion, the Mach 12 corridor, the
surge climb out of the atmosphere, and the loads: 500 g, -400 g, a 2,000 deg/s roll, then post-stall.

On the military board it flies the last sortie, a hypersonic pass: three airfields recorded from 7,000-14,000 m at
no less than 1,940 kt of ground speed (1,000 m/s), each kept ahead, in view and within 10 km for two seconds, then the
extraction gate at 9,000 m (`src/military_missions.h` HypersonicRecon). The hive's sensor model measures ground speed
for it, as for the XR-30's and the XR-50's sorties, so a dive doesn't count, and its sites are placed inside the
sensors' 10 km reach from the insertion altitude.
