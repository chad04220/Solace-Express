# The XR-50 Morpheus

The fifth research craft, and the one the others lead to. The XR-30 vectors its thrust, the XR-40 hovers on its pods;
the XR-50 does what each does and goes where neither can: it takes off vertically, swings its wings back past the
speed of sound, crosses Mach 7 on its scramjets in the corridor between too thick and too thin, and lights a rocket
that carries it out of the atmosphere. No one is aboard: it pulls loads no body survives, so it is flown from a ground
station, and it is twice the XR-40 in everything that makes a research craft aggressive. This note is the design and
the record of the decisions behind it. Where it and the code disagree, the code wins: fix the note in the same change.

## What it is, in a page

- **A waverider with swing wings.** A flat-bottomed forebody whose chines ride its own shock, a thick 66-degree glove
  from the chines to the wing pivots, and outer panels that sweep from 16 degrees (take-off, hover, landing, slow
  flight) back to 68 (supersonic and beyond). Span 15.5 m forward, 9.1 m swept. A V-tail at 48 degrees, all-moving.
- **Three engines in two.** Two combined-cycle engines: a turbofan with reheat to Mach 3.3, whose inlet doors then
  hand the air to a ramjet through Mach 5, and past Mach 4.3 the combustion goes supersonic, a scramjet to Mach 7.
  Between them, under the spine, a rocket with its own 3,400 kg of liquid oxygen.
- **VTOL.** A shaft-driven lift fan behind the camera bay (840 kN), the two nozzles swivelling 95 degrees down behind
  the centre of gravity, and roll posts under the gloves. The fan's doors open into the airflow above and below.
- **Thrust vectoring and reaction control.** The nozzles vector 20 degrees in pitch and yaw. High and slow, where the
  air no longer steers it, reaction jets do.
- **Fly-by-wire that knows what it has.** It commands rates, and each moment comes from whatever can make it now: the
  surfaces as far as the dynamic pressure allows, then the nozzles, then the fan and the posts, then the jets.
- **Remotely piloted, and twice the XR-40.** Its thrust for its weight, its pull and its push, its roll rate, each
  measured on both in the same air: 7.5 times its weight in reheat, 160 g and -136 g, 800 deg/s. A blown wing
  (supercirculation) gives it the lift for those loads; a 600 g structure carries them.
- **A ground station for a cockpit.** Its cockpit view is the room it is flown from: a desk, a bank of monitors that
  show its cameras with the remote pilot's symbology, a side stick and a throttle.
- **Heat as the limit.** The leading edges are carbon-carbon. Fly too fast too low and they burn through.

## Decisions

### One index, one flight model hook

The XR-50 is the roster's stable index 16 (`kMorpheus`), `special` 3, engine code 7. Every behaviour of its own
lives in `src/aircraft_morpheus.cpp`; `Plane::substep` calls `morpheusAero` for its aerodynamics and
`morpheusThrust` for its thrust and moments. `thrustAt`, `fuelFlowAt` and `liftThrustMax` ask the same engine
model, so the autopilot, the fuel planner and the learned envelope fly the same machine the pilot does.

### The wing sweeps itself

The schedule holds the wing at 16 degrees below Mach 0.72 and sweeps it to 68 degrees by Mach 1.45, at 8 degrees a
second (an F-14's rate). With the lever down for the hover, or the gear down below 120 m/s, it stays forward. Its
lift follows DATCOM's slope for the aspect ratio and half-chord sweep it has at the moment, Ackeret's supersonic
theory above the drag rise, and beneath both the waverider's body lift. Swept, its span loading is worse and its
drag rise later: the sweep is a real trade, not a decoration.

### Thrust from captured dynamic pressure

The ram and scram thrust is a coefficient on the dynamic pressure the inlets capture (1.8 m²), the fuel-air ratio
set by the throttle, and the combustors held to their pressure limit of 85 kPa. That cap is what keeps it honest
down low: deep in the air the thrust stops growing and the drag doesn't, so it runs out of thrust long before the
heat ends it. A scramjet's coefficient falls with Mach (it is past Mach 7 that thrust and drag balance), which puts
its top speed where the real ones' design points are, and puts that speed above 27 km.

### The heat corridor

The leading edges settle at the lower of the air's recovery temperature and the temperature where their glow
radiates away the Sutton-Graves heating of a 5 cm radius, over a 20 s thermal lag. Warning at 1,550 °C, limit at
1,650 °C; time over the limit accumulates and burns through. The scramjet corridor is therefore real: Mach 7 at
24 km kills it in under two minutes, the same speed at 29 km is fine. Its ENGINES and LEAD EDGE tiles say which
side of it the pilot is on.

### VTOL that balances

The fan sits 5 m ahead of the centre of gravity, the nozzles 7.4 m behind it. The lever swivels the nozzles as the
fan's clutch engages, never further than the fan is in, so the two come in together and the hover is balanced in
pitch at every setting. The clutch engages only below Mach 0.45 (its doors open into the airflow), and reheat is
inhibited with the nozzles off their aft stop. In the hover the fan's inlet vanes trade its thrust for pitch and the
roll posts work differentially for roll.

### Control allocation

The fly-by-wire asks for moments and gets them in order: the control surfaces as far as the dynamic pressure gives
them; the nozzles' collective pitch, differential pitch and yaw, solved as a damped least-squares problem within
their 20 degrees (Cramer's rule on a 3×3); the fan and posts in the hover; and the reaction jets when the dynamic
pressure is under 9 kPa above 12 km. The authority is real, so it goes soft where the air is thin and slow until the
propulsion takes over, as the real ones do.

### No one aboard: twice the XR-40

The XR-50 flies loads no body survives, so it carries no pilot; it is flown from a ground station (below). That is also
the brief: twice the XR-40 in every measure of aggression, each measured on both in the same air
(`xr50_flight_test`). Its engines are fiction as the XR-40's pods are: 525 kN each rated, for the fuel a real engine
of their size burns at the same throttle setting (their thrust-specific consumption a fifth of a real one's), so a
full-reheat minute costs what it did and cruise, at a sliver of throttle, lasts 29 hours. The ramjets and scramjets
keep their 85 kPa combustor limit, so the hypersonic corridor and the heat that bounds it are as they were. The lift
fan and the roll posts scale with the engines (0.8 and 0.06 of their rated thrust), the rocket doubles to 540 kN and
the reaction jets triple. Without a pilot's seat, displays and life support it is 1.5 t lighter (11.3 t empty).

### Supercirculation

A wing's lift for its weight bounds its pull at a given dynamic pressure, and the XR-50's wing loading is half again
the XR-40's: on lift alone it pulled fewer g than the XR-40 in the same air. So its wing is blown: the engines' bleed
leaves slots over the rounded trailing edges of the wing and the flaperons (circulation control, a Coanda jet), and
the whole lift curve rises to 4.7 times a plain wing's at full dry power - 1 + 3.7 times the blowing, which runs from
0.4 at idle to 1 at full dry power and is nothing with the engines out. The flaperons are blown too, one side against
the other, which multiplies their roll power the same way. Past Mach 2.5 the slots close (by Mach 4, against the
heat), where the forebody's compression lift takes over. The supersonic drag of making lift falls with the blowing (it
makes its lift at a shallower angle). The ground station's telemetry strip shows the load against the
structure.

### Limits, and the gate past them

The pitch command is the rate of a 160 g pull up and away (twice the XR-40's 80), 4 to 12 rad/s, and the roll and yaw
rates are twice its own: 14 and 3.6 rad/s. Its rate loops are twice as stiff. The one load limit is the airframe's:
the rate that holds 95% of the 600 g structure either way, less what it is over by. The angle of attack is held at 32
degrees, where the wing and the controls are whole, until the stick is pulled hard against its stop: through the gate
it goes post-stall to 90 degrees, held there by the nozzles when the tail can no longer do it. At these rates a limit
met head-on is a limit overshot, so the limiter closes the angle's own rate (the body's over the flight path's) as it
nears a limit, to what the pitch authority it has at that moment can stop there. The nozzles' actuators slew at 180
deg/s, stop to stop in under a quarter of a second, and their allocation is solved in double precision (at full
power its terms pass a float's range).

### The shape

The body is its own signed distance field (`src/shaders/morpheus_sdf.glsl`), all of it behind `HAS_MORPHEUS`, so no
other aircraft's program carries it. Moving parts (IDs 51 to 57): the swing wing and its flaperon, the V-tail halves,
the swivelling nozzles, the fan's upper door, the fan, its lower doors. Materials 150 to 159: the pearl skin over
black tiles beneath, the carbon-carbon edges that glow with their temperature, the gold-filmed canopy, titanium
nozzles, the amber strips, the duct interiors, the rocket's bell, the hot sections, the fan's blades, the wingtip
lenses.

The lifting body ends at the engines. Behind the nozzles' bearing rings (7.45 m) only two things go on: a narrow boat
tail between the engines, ending at the rocket's bell, and the V-tail's root fairings over the nozzles. Each nozzle
stands free, with 6 cm of clearance all round. Down each nozzle you see the afterburner's bore, lined in heat-stained
metal, then the flame holder (two V-gutter rings on four spraybar struts), the turbine's tail cone and the last
stage's face with its 36 blades. They glow dull red at idle and orange with the spool, and the reheat lights them.
(Until 4.3 the body ran on to 9.2 m, past the nozzles' exits, so the nozzles lay buried in it. Their bores came out of
the skin as ragged scoops into the empty inside.)

The canopy is an elliptical section pitched along the forebody's own top line rather than a level ellipsoid: it
leaves the nose's line with no step, rises at a 20-degree windscreen to stand 0.26 m over it above the pilot, and
runs back into the spine's flat top at under 5 degrees. Its aft fairing passes over the lift fan, so the fan's upper
door carries that part of it (as the F-35B's does).

### The ground station

Its cockpit is the room it is flown from (`morpheus_sdf.glsl mapMorpheusStation`), drawn round the eye where a cockpit
would be: a sealed operations room 2.7 m across, dark acoustic panels, two light panels overhead. The operator sits at
a desk before a bank of five monitors (`research_cockpit_layout.glsl morpheusPane`, each facing the eye): the main one
ahead (the nose camera), one either side angled in at 57 degrees, a chin monitor in the desk's console looking
forward and down (for the hover and the landing), and a telemetry strip above (the uplink's signal, the load against
the structure, the engines' mode). A side stick on the desk to the right, the throttle in its quadrant to the left,
pedals under the desk, a five-spoked chair, a rack of the uplink's equipment beside it, the programme's sign over the
bank. Its materials are 160 to 175.

The camera monitors are windows, as the research cockpits' displays are (`cabin_windows.glsl`: the four panes cut
through the room in the depth pre-pass and the material pass, so the world drawn first shows through), and each has
the remote pilot's symbology on its glass (`plane_screens.glsl morpheusHud`): the main monitor's boresight, flight-path
marker, horizon and pitch ladder, heading tape, speed and altitude, Mach, load and throttle, the uplink and its
latency, a recording lamp; the side cameras' horizon and heading; the chin camera's radar altitude and its VTOL
state. The glass's own fragment is kept where the symbology is lit; what lies behind the window stays cut. With the
cameras on (SCREENFEEDS) the monitors show feed rig 3's pictures, the same panes as windows. Looking at a side, chin
or telemetry monitor magnifies it; the main one is the view ahead and never is. The room lights itself from its
monitors (the sky ahead, the ground under the chin camera), its light panels and its amber accent strips.

## Flying it

- **F / V** run the nozzles down and up. Below Mach 0.45 the lift fan comes in with them.
- **X**, or a double tap of the brake, lights the rocket and puts it out.
- The wing sweeps itself; the tiles show where it is.
- To reach Mach 7, climb as you accelerate: 18 km by Mach 5, 27 km by Mach 7.
- Pull the stick through its stop for post-stall flight.

The research terminal's four cards train each of these: vertical flight and conversion, the scramjet corridor, the
edge of space, and the loads only a craft with no one aboard can pull - 160 g, -80 g, a 760 deg/s roll, then
post-stall. Nyx calls the cards, their steps, the engine modes, the rocket and the nozzles (clips
from the voice pack's Kokoro recipe, each checked by unprompted speech recognition).

On the military board it flies the last sortie, a supersonic sweep: three airfields recorded from 5,000-8,000 m at
650 m/s or more of ground speed. (The sensor's 10 km range keeps it out of the stratosphere: past that, the line of
sight it checks every step grows too costly.)
