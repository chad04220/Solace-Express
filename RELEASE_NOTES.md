## What's new
### The coast
- No more sea water in the middle of the land. The terrain's detail had dug 14 hollows below sea level well inland, up to 30 m deep and 1.4 km across (near Summit Pass, Kettle Lake, Northpoint and the volcano). The flat sea filled them, so pools of sea water sat in fields and forests, and an aircraft landing there "ditched in the sea". Each hollow is now lifted, with a smooth rise round it, into an ordinary valley floor with grass, fields and forest. Runway grounds and approach paths are never raised.
- Low beaches, spits and islands no longer sink under the sea in the distance and rise out of it as you come closer.
- Beaches are textured sand at a natural brightness, darker and glossier where the waves run up. Before, they blew out to a flat white in the sun. Palm Bay's sandy grounds are a smooth sand-and-grass mix instead of a repeating pattern of dots.

### Forests
- New firs and spruces. Each tier of branches is a star of drooping branch tips with notches between, ragged at the edges. Up close, every branch carries a flat spray of needles. Before, they were stacks of smooth paper cones.
- No two trees alike. Every tree turns its crown round the trunk by its own amount, is pushed a little out of round and leans its own way. One shape per species used to read as one tree copied across the hills. The shadows match.
- Pine crowns read as tufts of needles instead of smooth green cushions, and their needle tufts are finer. Conifers no longer turn autumn orange with the broadleaf trees.

### Water
- From the air the sea no longer shows one wave pattern stamped across it. Each wave band's crossing set now has its own size, so the two never line up again within sight. The wind waves and ripples come and go in patches about a kilometre across, with whitecaps gathering where it gusts.

### The cockpit
- More of the world ahead. The glareshield over the panel was a deep slab whose far edge sat only 2-4 degrees below the horizon. It is now a thin hood that slopes down to the windscreen, giving 4-6 degrees more view over the nose in every cockpit. The compass and the panel light strip sit on the new hood.

### Autoland
- The GPS autoland now uses the same rule the career dispatches by. It no longer takes the Starling or the Mantis onto Harlan Farm's grass or Palm Bay's sand, where they overran.
- A new stopping estimate, measured from 290 autopilot landings: the aim point plus the float and roll at the touchdown speed for the weight now, the field's air, any tailwind and the surface. The old one was more than twice the props' real distance and short of the jets'. It was also capped at 90% of the runway, so it never refused anything in still air.
- The XR-30 Specter now needs 1,400 m of hard runway. It was listed as needing 250 m on any surface, so the autoland took it to small fields where it overran, hit trees or collapsed its gear.
- The approach must stay on the chart, including the turn back onto the final. A go-around that runs on toward the edge turns back for the field. A passenger Starling at Palm Bay used to fly off the edge of the map and be lost.
- In the complete stress matrix (every aircraft at every field, three winds, two starts, both flying styles), every case now lands or is refused with a reason: 0 failures in 2,112 cases, against 74 before.

### Controls
- On-screen prompts name the device you're using. With a controller, the first lesson says "Press D-PAD LEFT to release the parking brake, then hold RT", not B (which lowers the flaps on a pad) and SHIFT. The other lesson hints, the engine-start tip and the GPS autopilot line ("Z engages") follow your bindings too. A key press switches the prompts back to the keyboard.

### Interface
- At large UI scales, the settings' render-resolution and frame-rate notes wrap instead of running off the panel. In the aircraft chooser, pointing at (or choosing) an aircraft whose name or reason was cut short shows them in full.
- A release is now published only after the Linux sanitizer tests pass on the same source, and those tests include the world cache.
