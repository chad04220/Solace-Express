## What's new
- Cockpit frame rate on the rasterizer. Three changes to the objects pass, which took 226 of the cockpit's 247 ms on the owner's RTX 3070: the cabin is drawn from its baked mesh and only the pixels over the moving controls march the field, and only as deep as the moving parts reach (the moving-part hull is built on the fine 6.25 cm lattice: its old 25 cm margin reached the pilot's eye, so every cockpit pixel marched); a march that lands on a surface the mesh already drew is dropped before it is shaded; and the airframe's own sun shadow and the sun through the cabin windows come from the airframe's shadow map, the field marched only on the map's edge texels and under the moving parts (that per-pixel shadow march alone was 72 ms). The same bounds apply in flight.
- The research craft's displays are windows again: the camera feeds, which rendered up to twelve extra views a frame (49 ms of the XR-40's 100 ms cockpit frame, 35 ms of the XR-30's), are gone except the bomb camera's picture on the floor pane while a bomb falls and the blast burns out. The screens' symbology goes with them; the HUD overlay stays. `SCREENFEEDS=1` brings the cameras back, now at a fraction of the view's density within a pixel budget; the research display pages redraw at 30 Hz.
- From Codex's performance review: the terrain grid is indexed (its height and normal samples run once per grid point, not once per triangle corner), the airframe shadow proxy looks up or marches a light's shadow only where the lighting pass will read it (the surface facing the light, inside its cone, lit above the floor), the camera feeds are timed as their own pass (F3, the benchmark, the analysis), and the analysis' per-pixel work counters run on the ray tracer only (they were never the rasterizer's). From its propeller review: the stopped blades' tip fade used an undefined smoothstep on both renderers.
- The research terminal has no button on the main menu any more: it opens on the held combo alone (both sticks pressed on the pad, U and I on the keyboard). Its boot screen is on the screen the instant the combo is held and holds on its last stage, saying what it is doing, while the previewed craft's shells bake and the launch site's scenery streams in; a bake no longer freezes the screen.
- Cabin edges. The sun visors, the grab handles and the lens rims are thinner than the cockpit mesh's 1.56 cm lattice and came off it as wavy, notched shapes (the visor's edge above the pilot's eye most of all). Those parts, and a ring round them, are now a separate patch whose pixels march the last few millimetres to the true surface, so their edges are the shape's own and stay straight at any resolution; the rest of the mesh pulls every vertex onto the surface in three steps within its own lattice cube rather than one.
